    Moon Base Trainer

    Keep a base alive at the real lunar south pole. Share your power, watch your air, and hide from solar storms. Every choice costs something, and the Moon you walk on is built from NASA data.

    Plays in your browser with no download or account.

      Debrief

      What you just learned:

        How to play

        1. You are the whole crew. Walk around the Habitat and use its stations. Click a station's label and you'll walk there.
        2. Watch three meters. Power comes from solar panels, and only while the Sun is up. Life Support is your air and water. Radiation dose is how much harmful radiation your body has soaked up. It goes up a little all the time, and a lot in solar storms.
        3. Nothing is free. The antenna, grow lights, robot and Boost all use power. Click your Power bar to see where it goes. Moon dirt can buy a regolith shield, repairs or extra oxygen, but not all three.
        4. Storms: keep the antenna on and Mission Control warns you early. Park the robot and hide in the Storm Shelter at the back. Once you have the Building tool, send the robot to dig moon dirt and build a regolith shield over the shelter. Every storm is sized from a real solar flare, and they change each time you play.
        5. Make it to sunrise with life support left and your mission goal met. Earn stars and research points to unlock new missions.
        6. Fill your Scrapbook. The first time you use a station or a machine, its page is added to your Scrapbook. Read it after the mission to see how the real one works, and earn 2 more research points.

        Keys: WASD or arrows to walk, Shift to run, Space to jump, drag to look, Q and R to turn the view, E to use, F for the chest light, 1 and 2 for your tools, B to build, T to speed up time (2x, then 3x), Esc to pause. The gear button pauses the game and holds the settings and the list of controls. The arrow on the right edge slides your mission out of the way and back.

        On a phone or tablet: use the joystick to walk (push it all the way to run), tap Use, Jump and Light, drag to look around, pinch to zoom, and tap a label to walk there. The gear pauses the game and holds the settings.

        Credits and NASA data

        Real NASA data in this game

          Game rates (power, air, dose) are made simple and sped up so a lunar night fits in a few minutes. The data cards after each mission give real NASA numbers, like how much oxygen an astronaut breathes and NASA's radiation limit. Each solar storm is sized from a real solar flare: the flare's class sets how strong the storm is and how long it lasts. The flares come live from NASA DONKI (the past 12 months) when your browser can reach it, otherwise from a list of real past flares. The game always says which one you got. Not every real flare sends a particle storm toward the Moon; in the game each one does.

          Photographs in the Scrapbook

          Every real-life photograph in the Scrapbook is from NASA. NASA does not endorse this game.

            Made by

            Team BaseRace for NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026: "Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer". 3D models are our own designs, built in Blender and inspired by real NASA hardware like the Foundation Surface Habitat and VSAT solar towers.

            Use of AI (Super Intelligence tools)

            We used Claude, an AI assistant made by Anthropic, to help write code, balance the game numbers, script the Blender models, check science facts and write text. Our team chose the design, directed the work and reviewed and tested the results.
