Moon Base Trainer

Keep a moon base running at the real lunar south pole. Share your power, watch your air, and hide from solar storms. The ground you walk on comes from NASA elevation data.

  • For ages 7 to 12
  • About 5 minutes a mission
  • No download, no account

You are the whole crew

Walk around the Habitat, use its stations, send a robot outside and go on moonwalks. Three meters tell you how the base is doing. Until sunrise, don't let Power or Life Support run out, and keep your radiation dose low.

Power 95% · draining
Solar panels only work while the Sun is up. Charge the battery by day and make it last through the lunar night.
Life Support 89%
This is your air and water. Run it on Eco to save power, or on Boost when you need air fast.
Radiation dose 6 of 100
It rises a little all the time and a lot in solar storms. Pile moon dirt on the Storm Shelter and hide inside.

Nothing is free. Everything that helps costs power, moon dirt, suit air or radiation dose, so you have to choose what to spend.

Three missions

  1. 1

    First Night

    You just landed. Charge the battery while the Sun is up, then make it last through the dark.

    Goal Reach sunrise with charge still in the battery

  2. 2

    Solar Storm

    Two solar storms are coming. Keep the antenna on for warnings from Earth and hide when the storm hits.

    Goal Keep your radiation dose low through two solar storms

  3. 3

    Ice and Greens

    Food is low and the cargo lander is late. Send the robot for crater ice and water the greenhouse.

    Goal Grow enough food to have meals left at sunrise

Play each mission as a Cadet, Explorer or Commander. Earn stars and research points to unlock the next one. Teachers and judges can open every mission at once from the bottom of the mission menu, or start with everything open.

A look around the base

The game's Moon comes from NASA data

Your base is on Connecting Ridge, one of the Artemis III candidate landing regions NASA named in 2022. The ground, the sunlight and the storms all come from NASA.

The ground you walk on
Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter LOLA elevation model, 5 metres per pixel. NASA PGDA product 78
Sunlight, Earth view and permanent shadow
LOLA south pole illumination maps. They set your solar power, your comms gaps and where the ice is. NASA PGDA product 69
Solar storms
Every storm is sized from a real solar flare in NASA's space weather database. The flare's class sets how strong the storm is and how long it lasts, and each playthrough draws different flares from the past 12 months. If your browser can't reach the database, the game uses a list of real past flares and says so. NASA DONKI
Radiation limit
Astronauts follow this crew health standard. NASA-STD-3001
Air and water numbers
These come from Ewert and Stromgren's 2019 NASA report. NASA Technical Reports Server
Ice in craters
In 2009 the LCROSS mission found water ice in a permanently shadowed crater. NASA LCROSS

We simplified the game's rates and sped them up so a lunar night fits in a few minutes. The data card after each mission gives the real numbers.

Make it to sunrise

  1. Use the stations. Click a station's label in the Habitat and you'll walk there.
  2. Watch the three meters: Power, Life Support and Radiation dose.
  3. Spend with care. The antenna, grow lights, robot and Boost all use power. Moon dirt can buy shielding, repairs or extra oxygen, but not all three.
  4. Hide from storms. Keep the antenna on and Mission Control warns you early. Park the robot and go to the Storm Shelter.
  5. Reach sunrise with life support left and your mission goal met.

Made for Space Apps 2026

Team BaseRace built Moon Base Trainer for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026. The challenge was "Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer". We designed the 3D models ourselves in Blender and based them on real NASA hardware like the Foundation Surface Habitat and VSAT solar towers.

Source code on GitHub

Use of AI

We used Claude, an AI assistant made by Anthropic, to help write code, balance the game numbers, script the Blender models, check science facts and write text. Our team chose the design, directed the work and reviewed and tested the results.

Open-source tools

three.js (MIT), Rapier physics (Apache 2.0), Vite (MIT). Fonts: Fira Sans, Overpass and Fira Code (SIL Open Font License). We drew the icons in the style of Feather (MIT).
